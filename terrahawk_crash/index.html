TerraHawk v1 crash during QuickTune

ArduPlane 4.7.1 · QuadPlane QUAD/X · QLOITER hover · log 00000008.BIN

QuickTune's pitch tuning set up an oscillation that overloaded a lift-motor mount until it broke in flight at about 10.5 m. The failure cut drive to all four lift motors and the aircraft fell almost freely. The oscillation came first; the mount failure followed.

What happened

296.5 s
QuickTune starts ramping Pitch P (and I). Pitch D was already left at 3.7× default by the previous stage.
~300 s
Oscillation begins: roll rocking, then a 4–6 Hz pitch oscillation that grows with the gain.
308.93 s
QuickTune's detector trips on the oscillation and cuts the gain.
~309.25 s
First mechanical sign: vibration leaves its normal range.
309.50 s
Mount breaks. Within ~50 ms current falls from 22 A to ~1 A while the autopilot keeps commanding full power.
311.08 s
Impact at ~12 m/s after 1.6 s of near free fall.

Times are seconds since boot, as in the log.

Why the tune caused it, not the reverse

  • The oscillation grew with the gain being ramped, and QuickTune's own detector tripped on it.
  • For the ~10 s of oscillation, vibration, trims, motor balance and structural stiffness all stayed normal.
  • The mount showed its first mechanical sign only ~0.25 s before breaking, at the highest load of the flight, after the oscillation had peaked.
  • The failed corner is almost certainly M4 (rear-right). It was already carrying about 2× the thrust of M1 because of an aft CG and a large yaw trim, and the oscillation drove it to ~97% output.

Contributing factors

  • No harmonic notch, which QuickTune depends on. No bidirectional DShot and no battery-voltage compensation either.
  • Tuned at ~11 m instead of the recommended ~3 m.
  • QWIK_ANGLE_MAX at 10° was never reached. At 5° it would have reverted the tune ~6 s before the failure.
  • A built-in 2:1 thrust imbalance put the extra load on M4 from the first hover.

Still open

  • Confirm which mount failed, and whether the fracture shows fatigue marks or a single overload break.
  • Find why all four motors lost drive at once. Check the shared ESC power and signal wiring (a brief 22 A current return mid-fall suggests an intermittent connection) and bench-test the ESCs.

Before the next flight

  • Stronger mounts on all four corners. Fix the CG and motor alignment so hover trims are near zero.
  • Set up bidirectional DShot with an RPM harmonic notch, and battery-voltage compensation.
  • Run QuickTune at ~3 m with QWIK_ANGLE_MAX at 4–5° and a slower ramp, with a hand on the revert switch.
  • Disarm immediately after any crash. This aircraft was picked up and carried while still armed.
